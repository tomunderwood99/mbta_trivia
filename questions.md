# MBTA Trivia

Add a question by copying this shape and using the next number. The page counts whatever is in this file.

```
1. **Question text**
   A. First choice  B. Second choice  C. Third choice  D. Fourth choice
   **Lines:** Red, Green
   **Answer: B — optional short label.** Explanation shown after the answer.
```

A fifth choice, `E.`, works the same way. **Lines:** is optional. Separate more than one line with commas. Leave it off when a question is not about a particular line.

1. **Which Red Line station features a set of interactive sound sculptures—named Pythagoras, Kepler, and Galileo—built to be played by pulling levers on the platform?**
   A. Park Street  B. Kendall/MIT  C. Porter  D. Davis
   **Lines:** Red
   **Answer: B — Kendall/MIT.** The Kendall Band, installed in 1987, was designed so riders could play three sculptures by pulling levers. Those are the sculptures' names.

2. **Which Red Line station is the deepest in the MBTA system?**
   A. Davis  B. Alewife  C. Porter  D. Harvard
   **Lines:** Red
   **Answer: C — Porter.** The outbound platform is about 105 feet (32 m) below the street, reached by a very long escalator. That is the deepest station on the T. It is not among the deepest subway stations in the world.

3. **Which MBTA line still runs vintage PCC streetcars from the 1940s in regular daily service?**
   A. Ashmont–Mattapan High Speed Line  B. Green Line E Branch  C. Silver Line SL1  D. Fairmount Line
   **Lines:** Mattapan
   **Answer: A — Ashmont–Mattapan High Speed Line.** It runs 1940s PCC cars as everyday working transit.

4. **Which two MBTA subway lines do NOT share a direct transfer station?**
   A. Red and Orange  B. Red and Blue  C. Blue and Green  D. Orange and Green
   **Lines:** Red, Orange, Blue, Green
   **Answer: B — Red and Blue.** Riders must walk (e.g. Bowdoin to Charles/MGH) or take a longer route.

5. **In what year did the Green Line Extension (GLX) into Somerville and Medford finally open?**
   A. 2016  B. 2019  C. 2022  D. 2024
   **Lines:** Green
   **Answer: C — 2022.**

6. **Which bridge carries Red Line trains across the Charles River?**
   A. Longfellow Bridge  B. Zakim Bridge  C. Harvard Bridge  D. Craigie Bridge
   **Lines:** Red
   **Answer: A — Longfellow Bridge.** It links Beacon Hill to Kendall Square.

7. **What major change happened to the Orange Line's route in 1987?**
   A. Moved underground through downtown  B. Moved from elevated tracks to the Southwest Corridor  C. Extended to Oak Grove for the first time  D. Converted from streetcar to heavy rail
   **Lines:** Orange
   **Answer: B.** The line left the old Washington St Elevated for the new Southwest Corridor.

8. **Why is the Blue Line's northern terminus called "Wonderland"?**
   A. A children's book author lived nearby  B. A Victorian-era theater  C. The shape of the station building  D. Wonderland Greyhound Park, itself named for an earlier amusement park on the site
   **Lines:** Blue
   **Answer: D.** The station opened in 1954 and was named for Wonderland Greyhound Park, which was operating then. The track took its name from Wonderland Amusement Park, which opened on the same site in 1906 and closed at the end of the 1910 season.

9. **What is the name of the MBTA's door-to-door paratransit service?**
   A. The RIDE  B. AccessLink  C. MobilityPlus  D. TransitAssist
   **Answer: A — The RIDE.**

10. **Which of these MBTA lines is powered entirely from overhead wires rather than a third rail?**
    A. Red Line  B. Orange Line  C. Green Line  D. Blue Line
    **Lines:** Red, Orange, Blue, Green
    **Answer: C — Green Line.** Red and Orange use a third rail. The Blue Line uses both, the reverse of a common mix-up: third rail in the older, tighter tunnels, including under the harbor, and overhead wire on the surface from Airport to Wonderland, where salt air and ice make a third rail a poor fit. Trains switch at Airport. The Green Line uses overhead wire for its whole route.

11. **Which Orange Line station opened in 2014 as the first new infill station in decades?**
    A. Wellington  B. Assembly  C. Sullivan Square  D. Community College
    **Lines:** Orange
    **Answer: B — Assembly.**

12. **Which MBTA line runs through the East Boston Tunnel, opened in 1904?**
    A. Red Line  B. Blue Line  C. Orange Line  D. Green Line
    **Lines:** Blue
    **Answer: B — Blue Line.** One of the first underwater transit tunnels built in the US.

13. **Which Green Line station, opened in 1897, is one of the oldest subway stations still operating in North America?**
    A. Boylston  B. Copley  C. Arlington  D. Symphony
    **Lines:** Green
    **Answer: A — Boylston.** Part of the original Tremont Street Subway.

14. **Downtown Crossing traces back to a set of older station names at the same site. Which of these names were among them?**
    A. Park Street and Boylston  B. Essex and Summer  C. Washington and Winter  D. State and Devonshire
    **Lines:** Red, Orange
    **Answer: C — Washington and Winter.** *(Note: this is the best fit among the choices, but it's a simplification. The Red Line platform was called "Washington" from 1915, while the Orange Line side actually had two separate platforms — "Winter" and "Summer," from 1908. All three names were consolidated into one "Washington" station in 1967, then renamed "Downtown Crossing" in 1987.)*

15. **Which commuter rail line shares Amtrak's electrified Northeast Corridor, even though its own trains are still diesel?**
    A. Fairmount Line  B. Providence/Stoughton Line  C. Franklin Line  D. Needham Line
    **Lines:** Commuter Rail, Providence/Stoughton
    **Answer: B — Providence/Stoughton Line.** Amtrak runs electric trains under the catenary. MBTA trains on the Providence section are still diesel locomotives on that same track. The Stoughton branch is diesel too and leaves the corridor at Canton Junction. The T is buying battery-electric locomotives that would use these wires, but they are not in service.

16. **How often does commuter rail run to Foxboro station?**
    A. Regular weekday trips, plus separate special event trains  B. Overnight only  C. 24/7  D. Special event trains only, on no regular schedule
    **Lines:** Commuter Rail
    **Answer: A.** A weekday pilot that began in September 2022 became permanent service on October 2, 2023. That launch schedule had 11 inbound and 10 outbound weekday trips. Gillette Stadium's current listing is 10 weekday round trips between Foxboro and South Station. Separate special event trains still run for Gillette Stadium concerts and games. *(Previously the station saw events-only service.)*

19. **In what year did Alewife open as the Red Line's northern terminus?**

20. **What do the letters B, C, D, and E stand for in the Green Line's branch names?**
    A. Nothing—just sequential letters  B. First letter of each terminus town  C. Order the branches were built  D. Bound, Central, Downtown, East
    **Lines:** Green
    **Answer: A.** They're simply sequential designations, not acronyms.

21. **Silver Line buses to the airport and Seaport used to be called "dual-mode." What did that mean?**
    A. Diesel only, always  B. Overhead electric in the Seaport Transitway; diesel on the surface and in the Ted Williams Tunnel  C. Battery only, always  D. Third rail power, like heavy rail
    **Lines:** Silver
    **Answer: B.** Trolley poles drew power between South Station and Silver Line Way. Diesel was used aboveground and through the Ted Williams Tunnel to Logan. Those buses were retired in July 2023. Replacements are extended-range hybrids: battery-electric in the Transitway, and diesel-hybrid everywhere else, including the Ted Williams Tunnel.

22. **South of which station does the Red Line split into Ashmont and Braintree branches?**
    A. Andrew  B. Savin Hill  C. JFK/UMass  D. North Quincy
    **Lines:** Red
    **Answer: C — JFK/UMass.**

23. **The Red Line's color name is often said to reference what?**
    A. The original streetcars' color  B. Harvard University's school color  C. The brick used in stations  D. The Freedom Trail
    **Lines:** Red
    **Answer: B.** The line originally terminated at Harvard Square (Harvard crimson).

24. **Where does the Orange Line's name come from?**
    A. A historic street name once used along its route  B. Yellow was tested first, and orange replaced it because it was easier to see  C. A Dutch colonial reference  D. The color of its elevated structure
    **Lines:** Orange
    **Answer: B.** Cambridge Seven Associates tried yellow, then switched to orange after user tests found orange easier for riders to see. Press accounts add that white station names did not show up well on yellow. Designer Peter Chermayeff has also described orange as a fourth color chosen for balance. Washington Street in the South End was once called Orange Street, but the MBTA's map history treats that overlap as a coincidence, not the source of the name.

25. **Why is the Blue Line called the "Blue" Line?**
    A. Its original paint scheme  B. A nod to the Blue Hills  C. The ocean and harbor it runs along  D. Its first operators' uniforms
    **Lines:** Blue
    **Answer: C.**

26. **The Green Line's color is often linked to Olmsted's Emerald Necklace. What does the MBTA's own map history say green was chosen for?**
    A. The color of its original streetcars  B. It was easy to tell apart on a map, not as a tribute to the parks  C. Fenway Park's outfield  D. Its underground tunnels
    **Lines:** Green
    **Answer: B.** The MBTA says the symbolic color legend is only partly true: red for Harvard, and blue for the harbor. Green and orange were chosen because they are easy to differentiate, and green was not chosen to celebrate the Emerald Necklace. Designer Peter Chermayeff has separately tied the color to those parks.

27. **Which commuter rail line is the direct descendant of the Fitchburg Railroad, incorporated in 1842?**
    A. Fitchburg Line  B. Worcester Line  C. Haverhill Line  D. Greenbush Line
    **Lines:** Commuter Rail, Fitchburg
    **Answer: A — Fitchburg Line.** The Fitchburg Railroad was chartered on March 3, 1842. Greenbush also goes back to an 1840s railroad (the Old Colony, chartered in 1844), so a question that only says "chartered in the 1840s" would not have a single answer. Worcester and Haverhill trace to 1830s charters.

28. **In what decade did the MBTA introduce its circular "T" logo?**
    A. 1940s  B. 1950s  C. 1960s  D. 1980s
    **Answer: C.** The logo debuted in 1965, designed by Cambridge Seven Associates.

31. **Which Orange Line station takes its name from a street that was itself named after a Revolutionary-era colonial family?**
    A. Ruggles  B. Stony Brook  C. Jackson Square  D. Roxbury Crossing
    **Lines:** Orange
    **Answer: A — Ruggles.** Named for Ruggles Street, itself named in 1825 for the Ruggles family of Roxbury, then a separate town.

32. **The Silver Line's first segment, opened in 2002, ran as bus rapid transit along which historic Boston street?**
    A. Boylston Street  B. Washington Street  C. Tremont Street  D. Atlantic Avenue
    **Lines:** Silver, Orange
    **Answer: B — Washington Street.** Once served by the elevated Orange Line, it became the Silver Line's first BRT route in 2002.

33. **The folk song that inspired the name of the MBTA's fare card told the story of a man trapped forever on a train because he couldn't pay what?**
    A. An entry fee  B. An exit fare  C. A late fee  D. A transfer charge
    **Answer: B — An exit fare.** "Charlie on the MTA" was written in 1949 for a Boston mayoral campaign. The Kingston Trio's 1959 recording is the famous version. In it, Charlie can't afford the nickel exit fare and rides forever.

34. **Before the MBTA was created in 1964, Greater Boston's transit system was run by an agency with a very similar acronym. What was it called?**
    A. Boston Transit Commission  B. Metropolitan Transit Authority (MTA)  C. Massachusetts Rail Board  D. Boston Elevated Company
    **Answer: B — Metropolitan Transit Authority (MTA).** This is the agency referenced in the classic folk song about Charlie.

35. **Which of the four subway lines is the shortest, end to end?**
    A. Red Line  B. Orange Line  C. Blue Line  D. Green Line
    **Lines:** Red, Orange, Blue, Green
    **Answer: C — Blue Line.** Runs from Bowdoin to Wonderland.

36. **Which subway line covers the most total route distance?**
    A. Red Line  B. Orange Line  C. Blue Line  D. Green Line
    **Lines:** Red, Orange, Blue, Green
    **Answer: D — Green Line.** The Green Line is about 26.7 miles across its branches. The Red Line is about 11.5 miles from Alewife to Ashmont and about 17.5 miles from Alewife to Braintree, about 22.5 miles combined. Orange is about 11 miles and Blue about 6.

37. **Which Orange Line station near the Theater District is known for having unusually narrow platforms?**
    A. Tufts Medical Center  B. Chinatown  C. Back Bay  D. Downtown Crossing
    **Lines:** Orange
    **Answer: B — Chinatown.** A legacy of the tunnel's original early-1900s construction.

38. **Which Orange Line/Green Line station takes its name from a historic marketplace that once operated in the area?**
    A. Haymarket  B. North Station  C. Bowdoin  D. State  E. Government Center
    **Lines:** Orange, Green
    **Answer: A — Haymarket.** Named for the historic hay and produce market, echoed today by the weekly Haymarket pushcart market.

39. **In 1967, the Blue Line platform at today's State station was folded into that station. What was the Blue Line platform originally named?**
    A. Devonshire  B. Winter  C. Essex  D. Milk
    **Lines:** Blue, Orange
    **Answer: A — Devonshire.** That platform opened in 1904 as Devonshire. The Orange Line side was already Milk (southbound) and State (northbound). On January 25, 1967, those names were consolidated as State.

40. **Which Green Line station is named after a famous 18th-century American portrait painter?**
    A. Arlington  B. Copley  C. Hynes  D. Prudential
    **Lines:** Green
    **Answer: B — Copley.** The station is named for Copley Square, which was named for the painter John Singleton Copley.

41. **South Station's grand Beaux-Arts headhouse first opened to the public in which year?**
    A. 1879  B. 1899  C. 1919  D. 1939
    **Lines:** Red, Silver, Commuter Rail
    **Answer: B — 1899.** It opened to the public on January 1, 1899, and became the country's busiest railroad station.

42. **What long-proposed but never-built project would directly connect North Station and South Station by rail?**
    A. The Crosstown Tunnel  B. The North-South Rail Link  C. The Central Connector  D. The Harbor Loop
    **Lines:** Commuter Rail
    **Answer: B — The North-South Rail Link.** Proposed for decades, never built.

43. **Roughly how many Massachusetts cities and towns fall within the MBTA's service area?**
    A. About 50  B. About 100  C. About 175  D. About 300
    **Answer: C — About 175.** The district was 175 communities by 1999 and 176 after Bourne joined in 2016. The FY2026 MBTA Advisory Board report counts 178 cities and towns, so "about 175" is still the right choice among these options.

44. **Which Silver Line route, extended in 2018, became notable for using a newly built dedicated bus bridge across a river?**

    **Lines:** Silver
    **Answer: C — SL3.** Extended service to Chelsea using a new busway and bridge over the Chelsea Creek.

    **Lines:** Green
    **Answer: B — Symphony.** Named for adjacent Symphony Hall, home of the Boston Symphony Orchestra.

51. **After the Orange Line moved to the Southwest Corridor in 1987, what was built along that new route, on land once cleared for a cancelled highway?**
    A. A new highway  B. A park  C. Additional Orange Line track  D. A garage
    **Lines:** Orange
    **Answer: B — A park.** The Southwest Corridor Park runs through the South End, Roxbury, and Jamaica Plain above and beside the relocated rail corridor. The old Washington Street Elevated was a different route: it was demolished, the street remained, and the Silver Line later replaced that service.

52. **The tunnel Silver Line buses use to reach Logan Airport was built as part of which major Boston infrastructure project?**
    A. The Big Dig  B. The Southwest Corridor project  C. The Red Line Northwest Extension  D. Urban Ring Phase 1
    **Lines:** Silver
    **Answer: A — The Big Dig.** The Ted Williams Tunnel was built as part of this project.

53. **What is the name of Boston's bike-share system, which has stations integrated near many T stops?**
    A. CitiBike  B. Bluebikes  C. Spin  D. HarborBike
    **Answer: B — Bluebikes.** Formerly known as Hubway.

54. **Before it was discontinued in 2023, what was the paper, magnetic-stripe fare ticket called — the disposable counterpart to the reusable plastic CharlieCard?**
    A. CharlieTicket  B. FareCard  C. QuickPass  D. RideSlip
    **Answer: A — CharlieTicket.** It could hold stored value or a pass and be used for many rides, so it was not a single-use ticket. Those insertable tickets were phased out in 2023. The name CharlieTicket now refers to a tappable plastic ticket, and the newer fare system also uses a short-term paper "temporary Charlie Card."

55. **The MBTA maintains its own dedicated police force with jurisdiction across the system. What is it generally known as?**
    A. MBTA Transit Police  B. Massachusetts State Police, Rail Division  C. Boston Metro Police  D. Commonwealth Security Force
    **Answer: A — MBTA Transit Police.**

56. **Heading north from Sullivan Square toward Malden, the Orange Line crosses which river?**
    A. Charles River  B. Mystic River  C. Neponset River  D. Chelsea Creek
    **Lines:** Orange
    **Answer: B — Mystic River.**

57. **Which MBTA commuter rail line has all of its stations inside Boston, unlike the rest of the network?**
    A. Franklin Line  B. Fairmount Line  C. Needham Line  D. Greenbush Line
    **Lines:** Commuter Rail, Fairmount
    **Answer: B — Fairmount Line.** Every stop from South Station to Readville is in Boston. The tracks clip a short stretch of Milton, including the site of the former Glenwood station, but there is no Milton stop. No other commuter rail line keeps every station in Boston.

58. **The Green Line D Branch was converted from a heavy commuter rail line to light rail in which decade, making it one of the earliest such conversions in the US?**
    A. 1930s  B. 1950s  C. 1970s  D. 1990s
    **Lines:** Green, Commuter Rail
    **Answer: B — 1950s.** The D Branch (Riverside) opened as a light rail conversion in 1959.

59. **The vintage streetcars used on the Mattapan Line are known by the acronym PCC. What does PCC stand for?**
    A. Presidents' Conference Committee  B. Public Commuter Car  C. Passenger Conveyance Corporation  D. Provincial Commuter Coach
    **Lines:** Mattapan
    **Answer: A — Presidents' Conference Committee.** The industry group that developed the standardized streetcar design in the 1930s.

60. **Unlike the four color-coded subway lines, what makes the Silver Line fundamentally different as a mode of transit?**
    A. It only runs on weekends  B. It's bus rapid transit, not rail  C. It's entirely underground  D. It's operated by a private company
    **Lines:** Silver
    **Answer: B — It's bus rapid transit, not rail.**
